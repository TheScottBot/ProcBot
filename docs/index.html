By the King, a proclamation

For two centuries, the Crown spoke to its subjects by proclamation: printed by the King's printer, read aloud in the market place and nailed up on church doors. Kings, queens, lords lieutenant and privy councils of England, Ireland and Scotland issued thousands of them, against tobacco, starch and coffee houses, for the apprehension of traitors, on plague, Jesuits and the price of ale.

It is now by the Inland plantation become promiscuous, and begun to be taken in euery meane Uillage, euen amongst the basest people.
(James I, on tobacco, 1619)

ProcBot reads them out in your server: 8,790 of them, from 1485 to 1714. 1,812 are in full, as printed, from the Text Creation Partnership's public-domain transcriptions of Early English Books Online. The rest are Robert Steele's summaries from his 1910 calendar of every proclamation he knew of. Each one links to its source.

Commands

  • /proclaim: a random proclamation, or /proclaim about:tobacco for one on a topic, by realm or monarch too
  • /wanted @someone: proclaim your friend a traitor, with a real proclamation for apprehending one
  • /decree thing: have a (made-up) proclamation against anything, in the Crown's own words
  • /onthisday: one given on this day of the year
  • /setup daily here: a Proclamation of the Day in a channel
  • /setup: what your server sees (needs Manage Server)
  • /help: all of the above, in Discord

Your server, your taste

These are the state's own words, and some of them are ugly: "papists" banished, bounties "dead or alive", "the detestable vice of buggery". Rather than cut them for everyone, every proclamation is tagged, and each server chooses what to keep: /setup religion level:mild, /setup lgbt level:off, and so on, or a preset from Tame to Unabridged. New servers start on a tasteful middle ground.

/setup here gives one channel its own rules: an Unabridged history channel, say, while the rest of the server stays Tame.
